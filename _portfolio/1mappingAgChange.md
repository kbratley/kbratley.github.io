Developed comprehensive monitoring systems to track farmland dynamics and support land management decisions across diverse environments. In Egypt, I created the first national-scale agricultural change mapping system, revealing critical patterns of expansion in desert regions and unexpected abandonment in traditional farming areas. This analysis provided actionable insights for policymakers addressing urbanization pressures and agricultural sustainability challenges. See the results in [this interactive app](https://kelseebratley.users.earthengine.app/view/egypt-land-cover-interactive-tool) for exploring Egypt's agricultural change patterns.

![GEE App Screenshot](/images/gee-app-screenshot.png)

 This work extended to other countries needing comprehensive agricultural mapping. Working with the International Monetary Fund, UN World Food Programme, and Digital Earth Africa, I developed Mozambique's first national agricultural extent map using advanced machine learning classification techniques.

![Moz Classificatoin Map](/images/2025Bratley-IMF-thumbnail.png)

Under the NASA SERVIR program, I extended similar approaches to Ghana, implementing time-series analysis algorithms to map agricultural systems and conducting field validation to ensure classification accuracy. This work involved developing methodologies that account for the complex characteristics of sub-Saharan agricultural systems, including mixed cropping patterns and integrated agroforestry practices.
